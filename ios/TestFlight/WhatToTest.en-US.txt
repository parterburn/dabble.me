New: Share Text, in an entry's Actions.

- Open an entry and swipe left on it (or swipe left on it in the Journal and tap Actions). There is a "Share Text" row above Delete. It opens iOS's share sheet with the entry's date and words.
- Choose Accessibility Reader there to have the entry read aloud. Copy, Print, Notes and the rest work too. After the first time, the destination you used should be near the front of the sheet.
- An entry that is only a photo has no Share Text row.

Holding an entry for its preview: there is no "Open" in the menu any more.

- Touch and hold an entry in the Journal: the preview comes up with Reflection, Edit and More under it, but no Open. Tap the preview itself to open the entry.
- The same for On This Day cards, a photo in Photos, and a written day in Stats or Jump to a Day: just the preview, which a tap opens.

Removed: reading a reflection aloud.

- The play button next to a reflection's X is gone. Everything else about a reflection (the follow-up chat, "Sum Up & Add to Entry") is as it was.

The bar over the keyboard while writing.

- Format (the "A") and Link now show only while words are selected. Select a word: both appear. Put the cursor somewhere without selecting: they go, leaving photos, hashtags and the lightbulb.
- Format is a short menu again: Bold, Italic, Underline. The slide-up formatting panel and strikethrough are gone.
- A link is added by selecting words first, or by pasting: a web address pasted by itself goes in as a link (tap it to see or change where it goes), and one pasted over selected words links those words. (Links are part of PRO; on a free account an address pastes as plain text.)

Hashtags row.

- When hashtags fit what you've written, an "Insert" button sits at the right of the row and stays there while the tags scroll. It adds all the highlighted ones in one go.
- While suggestions are still being looked for and none are known yet, the three dots are in that same spot.
- The first time you tap Insert, a tip says you can touch and hold the tag button. The hold tags only the words in your entry that match hashtags you already use: it doesn't ask for suggestions or add anything at the end.

Entry template: the first time you tap "Use Template", a tip says you can touch and hold the lightbulb instead.

No camera button when the camera is turned off for Dabble Me.

- In the iPhone's Settings, Dabble Me, turn Camera off. Start an entry: the bar over the keyboard should have the photos button but no camera button, and the photo row's "add more" should go straight to your library.
- Turn Camera back on: the camera button should be back.

Cancel while writing: the question about your draft can now be seen.

- Write a few words, open a row over the keyboard (hashtags, or the lightbulb), and tap Cancel. The keyboard and the row should go down, with "Save Draft" and "Discard" in view.
- Tap outside the question (or "Keep Writing", where it's shown): the keyboard should come back.

Removed: speaking an entry.

- The microphone button over the keyboard is gone (the keyboard's own dictation is still there). The bar ends at the lightbulb.
- Dabble Me no longer asks for the microphone.

Fixed: a template lost its blank lines and empty bullets.

- Settings, Entry Template: write headings with an empty bullet or a blank line under each. Go back and in again: the empty bullets and blank lines should still be there. (A template saved by an earlier build needs its empty lines typing in again.)
- Use the template in an entry (the lightbulb row, or holding the lightbulb): the empty places come with it, and the cursor starts in the first one.
- Fill in part of it, leave the app, and come back to the draft: the places you hadn't filled yet should still be there.
- Save the entry: empty bullets and blank lines you left unfilled are dropped from the saved entry, as before.

The lightbulb row while writing.

- Its heading is now "WRITING IDEAS".
- While the prompt is being written the row takes one line. When the prompt arrives, the top of the row should rise to fit it; the calendar events under it should not move.
- At most three of the day's calendar events are listed.

Fixed: gaps around the photo of a long entry before "Show more" is tapped.

- On Today and in the Journal, find an entry long enough to show "Show more" that has a wide photo or collage. The photo should sit right under "Show more" with no empty band above or below it, and the preview should show its full number of lines.
- Tap "Show more": it should look as it did. Tall photos should still stop at the same height as before.

Spacing under an open entry.

- Open an entry with no photo that has On This Day under it. The gap from its last line to the dividing line should look the same as the gap from that line to the "On This Day" heading.
- Check it for an entry ending in plain writing, one ending in dated tags (like "#Leo 9yrs"), and one ending in songs.

Fixed: the New Entry control had no icon.

- In Control Center's Add a Control list (search for Dabble Me), and wherever you've put the control, it should show a small serif "me". If one you added earlier is still blank, remove it and add it again.

Reflection: "Sum Up & Add to Entry" is more careful.

- It now adds each question you answered with what you said to it, one after the other, written out as proper sentences.
- A question you were asked but didn't answer should not be in it.
- Nothing your entry already says should be repeated, even if your answer went over it again. Check that what's added is all new.
- Try it after one answer, after several, and after leaving the last question unanswered.

The "?" button over the keyboard is now a lightbulb. It does what it did.

New: an entry template.

- Settings, App, Entry Template. Write a few headings or questions; the "A" button formats them (bold, lists and so on) as in an entry. Go back and in again: it should still be there.
- With "Start New Entries with It" off: start an entry and tap the lightbulb. The row should say "Tap to generate prompt" with a "Use Template" button at the right. Tap the button: your template goes in where the cursor is, with its formatting.
- Tap the lightbulb again and tap "Tap to generate prompt": a prompt is written, and the "+" takes the button's place.
- Touch and hold the lightbulb button: the template goes straight in, without the row opening.
- Turn "Start New Entries with It" on and start a new entry: it should open with the template in it, and Save should stay off until you write something. The lightbulb row is then as before, with no "Use Template" button.
- Empty the template in Settings: everything should be as it was before this build.
- The template is on this device only: it does not follow you to another phone or the website.

The rows over the keyboard (prompt, hashtags, link, photos) are on the warm paper color.

- Open each row while writing. The row and the buttons under it should sit on paper, apart from the white page you are writing on.
- Look at the keyboard's rounded top corners while a row is open: paper should show behind them, not the page's color. Check dark mode too.
- With no row open, the buttons float over the page as before.

Fixed: the Format button while writing did nothing.

- Tap the "A" button over the keyboard. The keyboard should go down and the Format panel (Bold, Italic, Underline, Strikethrough, lists) should take its place.
- Close it with its X or by swiping it down: the keyboard and the buttons should come back, with your selection as it was.

The "?" row is tighter.

- With calendar events that day, the prompt should sit close over them, with a line between the prompt and the first event, and no empty gap.
- While the prompt is being written the events may move up a little when it arrives. Say if that is distracting.

An entry without a photo has more room under its last line.

- Open an entry with no photo that has On This Day under it. The gap under the last line of writing should match the gap above the "On This Day" heading.
- An entry with a photo should look as it did.

New: speak your entry.

- Open the composer. On iOS 26 there is a microphone button at the far right of the bar over the keyboard, after the "?" button. (On iOS 18 it isn't there.)
- Tap it. The first time, allow the microphone; it may then say it is downloading what it needs, once. After that a "Listening" row appears over the buttons.
- Talk for a few sentences. The words show in the row as you say them, and go into the entry a sentence at a time, where the cursor is.
- Tap Done, or the microphone again. The last words should land in the entry and the row should close.
- Check the punctuation and names against what you said. Is it better than the keyboard's dictation?
- Type some words, put the cursor in the middle of them, and speak: the new sentence should go in at the cursor with a space before it.
- Start speaking and tap Save straight away: the last sentence should be saved with the rest.
- While it is listening, open the hashtag or "?" row, or swipe the keyboard down: listening should stop and keep what you said.
- Try it in airplane mode after the first use. It should still work; nothing you say leaves the phone.
- Try a long entry (a minute or two of talking) and say whether the phone gets warm or the words fall behind.
- If you turn the microphone off for Dabble Me in Settings and tap the button, the row should say so and offer Settings.

The buttons over the keyboard while writing are olive now, not black. Check them in light and dark mode.

Switching rows above the keyboard should never leave an empty band over your writing.

- While writing with a photo attached, tap "?" (the prompt), then the photos button, then "?" again, a few times, quickly and slowly. Each row should replace the last cleanly. Watch for a blank strip between the row and your text, and say which switch caused it if you see one.
- Try the same with the hashtag and link rows, and on a day with many calendar events.
- After a row closes, the last lines of your entry should scroll clear of the bar, and you should be able to tap text just above the bar.

The "?" row while writing is rearranged.

- The prompt is now on top and the day's calendar events are under it. With events, the heading reads "A PROMPT FOR YOU / AGENDA"; without, just "A PROMPT FOR YOU".
- The whole row scrolls as one piece on a busy day, and its content sits flush under the line above it, with no gap where events get cut off.
- Open it and watch while the prompt is being written: the events underneath should not jump when the prompt appears. (A prompt longer than three lines can still nudge them.)
- Settings, Calendars: the "W" of "When you're writing…" should no longer be clipped at the top left.

A prompt or calendar event now goes in where your cursor is, not at the end of the entry.

- Write three paragraphs. Put the cursor in the middle one, tap "?", and tap an event or the prompt. It should appear in bold right under that paragraph, with the cursor on a new line beneath it. The third paragraph should still be below.
- Put the cursor on an empty line in the middle of the entry and do the same. It should land on that empty line, not push down to the bottom.
- With the cursor at the very end, and in an empty entry, it should behave as before: the heading, then the cursor under it.

How long ago something was now follows one rule everywhere.

- Up to 60 days back it's counted in days ("36 days ago"). Past that it's years and months to the nearest month ("2 months ago", "12 years, 5 months ago"), never weeks.
- Check it reads the same for one entry in each place: under the title when the entry is open, on its photo's band, on Today's Rewind, on the widget, and in the push reminder.

Reflection has three changes.

- The send button is an up arrow.
- There's a play button next to the X. Tap it and the reflection is read aloud; tap again to stop. It should play with the ring switch on silent, stop when you close the sheet, and quiet any music while it speaks. How does the voice sound? (A better one can be downloaded in Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, Voices.)
- "Sum Up & Add to Entry" now gives you the question you were asked, reworded so it makes sense by itself, in bold, and your answer under it, cleaned up. Check that the answer is what you said, in your words, with nothing added or interpreted, and that typos or dictation slips are fixed. Try it after one answer and after several. Then add it and look at the entry.

The Format button now opens Apple's formatting panel, and entries can have strikethrough.

- While writing, select some words and tap the "A" button. A short panel should slide up with Bold, Italic, Underline, Strikethrough and list buttons, and nothing else (no fonts, colors or alignment).
- Try each of the four styles. The buttons should light up to match the selected words. Does the selection stay visible while the panel is open?
- Tap a list button with a few lines selected: they should become "• " bullets or "1. 2. 3." items. Tap it again: back to plain lines.
- With nothing selected, turn on Bold, close the panel and type: the new words should be bold.
- Close the panel with its X, and by swiping it down. The keyboard should come back both times.
- Save an entry with struck-through words, then reopen it in the app and on dabble.me: the strikethrough should still be there in both.
- Select words, tap Format in the pop-up menu, then More…: the same short panel, without the list buttons.

No more second splash after unlocking.

- With the Face ID lock on, open the app and unlock. The typed "Dabble me." should stay exactly where it is, without the Face ID symbol, until your journal is ready, then fade to it. The square "me" logo should not flash up in between.
- From a cold start on a slow connection, the logo should hold no longer than about two seconds after unlocking.
- The "me" logo should still cover your journal in the app switcher (swipe up and pause), and when Control Center or a notification is pulled down over the app.

Choosing a suggested hashtag no longer moves the cursor.

- While writing, open the hashtags row and tap a suggestion for a word that's already in your entry (say "Leo"). A "#" should go in front of that word, and the cursor should stay exactly where you were typing.
- Try it with the cursor before the word, after it, and in the middle of a different line, and with some text selected.
- A suggestion for a word that isn't in the entry still adds "#tag" at the end and puts the cursor after it.

Writing an entry:
